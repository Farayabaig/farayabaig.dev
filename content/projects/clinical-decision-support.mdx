## The problem

NCCN guidelines define how cancer should be treated, but they are long, branching documents written for people. Checking a treatment plan against them by hand takes time and expertise, and a missed deviation matters.

## Key engineering decisions

<Decision title="A knowledge graph, not a black box">
Clinicians need to see **why** a plan was flagged. Encoding guidelines as a graph made every decision traceable to the guideline path it came from.
</Decision>

<Decision title="Interactive by design">
Making the graph interactive let clinical staff explore the guidelines themselves, not just receive flags.
</Decision>

<Decision title="Build the knowledge base once, reuse it">
The graph doubles as a **reusable knowledge base** for downstream clinical AI work, so the effort of encoding the guidelines keeps paying off.
</Decision>

## Results

- Treatment plans automatically validated against NCCN guidelines, with deviations from clinical standards flagged.
- A machine readable clinical knowledge base ready for future AI work.

## What I would do next

Pair the graph with an LLM that explains each flag in plain language, keeping the graph as the source of truth so the explanation can never contradict the rule.
